Comprehensive Metabolic Panel (CMP) and Basic Metabolic Panel (BMP)

The metabolic panel measures substances in your blood that indicate how well your kidneys, liver, and other organs are working. The BMP includes 8 tests; the CMP adds 6 more liver and protein tests.

Glucose
Normal range (fasting): 70-100 mg/dL
Glucose is your blood sugar level. High glucose (hyperglycemia) above 100 mg/dL fasting may indicate prediabetes (100-125 mg/dL) or diabetes (126+ mg/dL). Chronically elevated glucose damages blood vessels, nerves, kidneys, and eyes. Low glucose (hypoglycemia) below 70 mg/dL can cause shakiness, confusion, and in severe cases, loss of consciousness. Common causes of low glucose include diabetes medications, skipping meals, and excessive exercise.

Blood Urea Nitrogen (BUN)
Normal range: 7-20 mg/dL
BUN measures waste product from protein breakdown filtered by kidneys. High BUN may indicate kidney dysfunction, dehydration, high protein diet, heart failure, or gastrointestinal bleeding. Low BUN may indicate liver disease, malnutrition, or overhydration. The BUN-to-creatinine ratio helps distinguish between kidney and non-kidney causes.

Creatinine
Normal range: Men: 0.7-1.3 mg/dL, Women: 0.6-1.1 mg/dL
Creatinine is a waste product from muscle metabolism filtered by kidneys. High creatinine usually indicates impaired kidney function. It can also be elevated in people with high muscle mass or after intense exercise. Low creatinine may indicate low muscle mass or liver disease.

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR)
Normal range: >60 mL/min/1.73m2
eGFR estimates how well your kidneys filter blood. Values below 60 for three months or more indicate chronic kidney disease. Stages: 60-89 mild decrease, 30-59 moderate decrease, 15-29 severe decrease, below 15 kidney failure requiring dialysis.

Sodium
Normal range: 136-145 mEq/L
Sodium helps regulate fluid balance, nerve function, and muscle contractions. High sodium (hypernatremia) may indicate dehydration, diabetes insipidus, or excessive salt intake. Low sodium (hyponatremia) may be caused by excessive water intake, heart failure, kidney disease, liver cirrhosis, or certain medications. Severe sodium imbalances can cause confusion, seizures, and coma.

Potassium
Normal range: 3.5-5.0 mEq/L
Potassium is critical for heart, muscle, and nerve function. High potassium (hyperkalemia) above 5.0 can cause dangerous heart rhythm problems and may be caused by kidney disease, certain medications (ACE inhibitors, potassium-sparing diuretics), or cell breakdown. Low potassium (hypokalemia) below 3.5 can cause muscle weakness, cramps, and heart rhythm problems, often from diuretics, vomiting, or diarrhea. Critical values below 2.5 or above 6.5 require immediate medical attention.

Chloride
Normal range: 98-106 mEq/L
Chloride helps maintain fluid balance and acid-base balance. Abnormal chloride usually mirrors sodium abnormalities. High chloride may indicate dehydration or kidney disease. Low chloride may indicate vomiting, heart failure, or metabolic alkalosis.

Carbon Dioxide (CO2/Bicarbonate)
Normal range: 23-29 mEq/L
CO2 in blood reflects acid-base balance. Low CO2 may indicate metabolic acidosis from diabetic ketoacidosis, kidney disease, or severe diarrhea. High CO2 may indicate metabolic alkalosis from vomiting, chronic lung disease, or certain medications.

Calcium
Normal range: 8.5-10.5 mg/dL
Calcium is essential for bones, muscles, nerves, and blood clotting. High calcium (hypercalcemia) may indicate hyperparathyroidism, cancer, excessive vitamin D, or certain medications. Low calcium (hypocalcemia) may indicate vitamin D deficiency, hypoparathyroidism, kidney disease, or magnesium deficiency. Severe imbalances can affect heart rhythm.

Total Protein
Normal range: 6.0-8.3 g/dL
Total protein measures albumin and globulin in blood. High total protein may indicate chronic inflammation, infection, or bone marrow disorders. Low total protein may indicate liver disease, kidney disease (protein loss), malnutrition, or malabsorption.

Albumin
Normal range: 3.5-5.5 g/dL
Albumin is the most abundant protein in blood, made by the liver. Low albumin may indicate liver disease, kidney disease (nephrotic syndrome), malnutrition, or chronic inflammation. It is also an indicator of overall nutritional status.

Total Bilirubin
Normal range: 0.1-1.2 mg/dL
Bilirubin is a waste product from red blood cell breakdown, processed by the liver. High bilirubin causes jaundice (yellowing of skin and eyes). Causes include liver disease (hepatitis, cirrhosis), bile duct obstruction, or excessive red blood cell destruction (hemolytic anemia).

Alkaline Phosphatase (ALP)
Normal range: 44-147 U/L
ALP is an enzyme found mainly in liver and bones. High ALP may indicate liver disease (bile duct obstruction), bone disease (Paget's disease, bone cancer), or can be normal during growth in children and pregnancy. Low ALP is uncommon but may indicate malnutrition or hypothyroidism.

Aspartate Aminotransferase (AST/SGOT)
Normal range: 10-40 U/L
AST is an enzyme found in liver, heart, muscles, and other tissues. High AST indicates tissue damage, most commonly liver damage from hepatitis, alcohol, medications, or fatty liver disease. Very high values (>1000) suggest acute hepatitis or drug toxicity. Mild elevations can also come from muscle damage or heart attack.

Alanine Aminotransferase (ALT/SGPT)
Normal range: 7-56 U/L
ALT is an enzyme more specific to the liver than AST. High ALT strongly suggests liver damage. Common causes include hepatitis, fatty liver disease, alcohol use, and medication side effects. The AST/ALT ratio can help determine the cause of liver damage.
